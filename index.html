01

Ashish Shah / Tatler Hong Kong, via Vogue India · Resized and converted to WebP

READ / INDIA & THE IMAGE

Photographer Ashish Shah writes about why the world needs to see and accept an ordinary India

Vogue India · Ashish Shah, as told to Aliza Fatma / 2 Oct 2026 · First-person essay · Free read

Ashish Shah describes unlearning photographic conventions built around white skin and picturesque India. For Alexander McQueen’s 2021 campaign, he chose Dehradun rooftops over spectacular locations. His account connects technical choices, trust with subjects and the right to recognise yourself in an image.

Why it’s included

Representation also sits inside lighting and location. Shah’s rooftops make that tangible: an ordinary setting can challenge an industry’s visual expectations. His account is especially useful for noticing assumptions hidden inside supposedly neutral decisions about how to make something look good.

02

Blackbird Spyplane · Resized and converted to WebP; no crop

READ / MEDIA & CRAFT

I used to get paid to hang out with famous people

Blackbird Spyplane · Jonah Weiner / 22 Sep 2026 · Substack essay · Free read

Jonah Weiner revisits two decades of celebrity profiles, from shadowing Jimmy Kimmel for three days to interviewing Drake. His account explains the access, observation and sentence-level craft behind written portraits, alongside the shrinking economics that once sustained them.

Why it’s included

Weiner asks for three kinds of access: an interview, time hanging around and time watching work happen. That distinction shows what a polished conversation can miss, and why a transcript and a reported portrait offer different kinds of understanding.

03

Ahana Gurung / Aabyari, via Condé Nast Traveller India · Resized and converted to WebP

READ / INDIA & CULTURAL SPACES

In Srinagar, a century-old home is now a cultural centre celebrating Kashmir’s craft and cuisine

Condé Nast Traveller India · Ahana Gurung / 6 Oct 2026 · Reported visit · Free read

At Aabyari, a restored Srinagar home, Ahana Gurung follows pashmina weaving, architectural restoration and a wazwan supplied by neighbourhood cooks. Houseboat carpenters helped restore the building; cookies come from Zaina Kadal. The cultural centre opened in April 2026.

Why it’s included

Pay attention to what the venue commissions from its neighbours. Those relationships give the restoration substance beyond its handsome interiors. The account makes craft visible as paid, continuing work, while leaving room to question how cultural experiences are packaged for visitors.

04

Museum of Art & Photography, Bengaluru · Resized and converted to WebP

GO / BANGALORE & ART

Living Botanicals

MAP Bengaluru · Bhavana Kumar & Kush Sethi / On view until 22 Nov 2026 · Free installation · Closed Mondays

Plants native to the subcontinent occupy MAP’s sculpture courtyard inside a pavilion designed by architect Bhavana Kumar. Gardener Kush Sethi tends the seasonal growth. The installation treats cultivation and changing conditions as part of the work itself.

Why it’s included

A useful stop after a gallery visit: the object on display will not hold still. Notice how the pavilion gives plants a structure without fixing their final form, and how the work depends on repeated, ordinary acts of care.
