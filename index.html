02 / ArticulationA hypothesis I believe in

Articulation is
the skill I’d bet on.

I believe articulation could become the most valuable skill as AI makes creation and execution easier.

Take “build a community.” Do we mean an audience, customers who talk to each other, or people who might actually become friends? Each answer asks us to make something different. I like stopping at a word like that before it becomes a brief, and finding language a team can test, question and act on.

03 / Working togetherStart with the decision

What can we
make clearer?

Bring a brief, a knotty question or an opportunity. Here are three kinds of decisions I can help with.

01

Brand strategy

“People know what we sell. What do we stand for?”

Find a position the brand can credibly take, with an argument and principles for what it says and does.

02

Creative strategy

“We have the objective. What is the thought?”

Turn the audience’s reality into a central thought, a message hierarchy and a brief the team can make from.

03

Content strategy

“We’re saying a lot. What should people come to us for?”

Set an editorial purpose, recurring topics, a recognisable voice and a clear job for each channel.

READ / CINEMA & CULTURE

Girish AD and the return of relatable cinema in Malayalam

The Indian Express · Nidheesh MK / 17 Sep 2026 · Reported profile · Free read

In this on-location profile, Girish AD explains why his films keep the songs, awkwardness and mixed-up geography of the Kerala he knows. Local references give viewers another conversation to have after the screening—and another reason to watch again.

Why it’s included

The telling detail is the village he went looking for and could not find: the pastoral film cliché had missed the mall down the road. A sharp account of cultural specificity, and the pleasure of recognising your own world.

02Thirst Behavior on Substack · Bodhi Landa
READ / TASTE & HOSPITALITY

The Production of Ease

Thirst Behavior on Substack · Bodhi Landa / 19 Aug 2026 · Essay · Free read

Landa and a New York Times critic ate at Bistrot Ha on the same night. Her table endured disastrous service; his felt wonderfully looked after. He uses the gap to examine how diners turn someone else’s labour into atmosphere, authenticity and taste.

Why it’s included

Two honest accounts of one room can disagree. Landa makes that tension productive: visible effort can feel intimate, yet hard work cannot excuse a bad experience. Worth reading for how carefully he keeps both perspectives in view.

READ / INDIA & EVERYDAY RITUAL

Inside five dargahs in Hyderabad and the beautiful traditions that sustain them

Vogue India · Mallik Thatipalli / 19 Sep 2026 · Reported essay · Free read

Thatipalli visits five Hyderabad dargahs and talks to people who return for comfort. Handprints, money wrapped in yellow cloth and photocopied legal papers make their worries tangible. The story follows the objects and gestures through which devotees express hope.

Why it’s included

A close look at how a place accumulates meaning through repeated, personal acts. The details reward attention, and the voices of regular visitors keep faith and belonging grounded in lived experience. Rahul Cheguri’s photographs are part of the pleasure.

04The Point · Sean Tatol
READ / ART & CULTURAL SCENES

After the Post-Avant-Garde

The Point · Sean Tatol / 2 Aug 2026 · Criticism · Long read

Tatol argues that the pursuit of online visibility weakens the small art scenes that develop their own standards. Moving through artists, galleries and exhibitions, he asks what remains when novelty loses its authority—and proposes a more expansive relationship with art history.

Why it’s included

The demanding read of this selection. Its useful distinction is between being new and speaking to the present. Read it alongside Girish AD’s profile: both complicate the assumption that cultural relevance requires an unfamiliar surface.

05
GO / BANGALORE

The Ocean Between Us

Bangalore International Centre · Diya Naidu & Nadia Milford / 30 Sep 2026 · 7–8:15pm IST · Ages 16+ · Free with RSVP, subject to availability

This movement theatre collaboration began as an Instagram residency and developed through work in India and Australia. Naidu and Milford use dance and writing to explore listening, identity and care. BIC hosts the performance in its gallery in Domlur.

Why it’s included

A chance to encounter ideas through bodies sharing a room. The project’s origin—two artists responding to difficult personal questions—makes it an intriguing extension of this edition’s interest in how people understand one another. RSVP through BIC; entry depends on availability.
