READ / INDIA & URBAN MEMORY

A gallery of memories: How Kolkata’s citizens are preserving the city’s Chinese heritage

Scroll · Sayari Debnath / 26 Sep 2026 · Reported feature · Free read

Inside Kolkata’s new Cheenapara Heritage Gallery, a 1960s restaurant menu, a Chinese-language newspaper and tooth-setter’s tools preserve the city’s Chinese history. Sayari Debnath also traces the community’s contraction after 1962 and the institutions it still maintains.

Why it’s included

The most revealing objects are ordinary records of work, food and association. Together they show preservation as more than saving buildings: it means keeping community spaces active, documenting who used them and refusing a city’s habit of treating residents as outsiders.

02Why is this interesting? · Mark Slavonia
READ / PUBLIC LIFE

The Pittsburgh Parallel Parking Championship Edition

Why is this interesting? · Mark Slavonia / 1 Oct 2026 · Substack essay · Free read

A family argument over the best parallel parker became a six-year-old Pittsburgh street championship. Mark Slavonia reports on its one-run format, volunteer stations and wonderfully blunt scoring system: parking time multiplied by distance from the curb.

Why it’s included

The delight comes from operational care, not spectacle. No preregistration, ten minutes from arrival to scorecard, simple public rules and friendly marshals turn an everyday competence into a ritual people want to perform together.

PLAY / INTERNET & MEMORY

Everything, Everywhere, All At Once

Jay Zuerndorfer & Ori · Tiny Awards 2026 finalist / 2026 · Archival pick · Interactive memorial · Free browser experience

Jay Zuerndorfer turns the days after losing Ori into a scroll through call logs, photographs and fragments of routine. The page moves from day zero to day twenty, ending after the layers of early grief have been worked through.

Why it’s included

A quiet example of interaction serving emotion. The scrolling asks for patience, while dated fragments resist the urge to make grief neat. It shows how a webpage can hold private memory without turning it into disposable content.

04
GO / BANGALORE & MUSIC

Pomp and Circumstance

Bangalore International Centre · V. Sriram / 11 Oct 2026 · 11am–1pm IST · Free with RSVP, subject to availability

Historian V. Sriram traces Carnatic musician Muthiah Bhagavatar across performance, scholarship and institution-building. Bhagavatar composed more than 400 songs, introduced around twenty ragas, led Trivandrum Music College and founded a Madurai gurukula in 1920.

Why it’s included

Go for a portrait of cultural work that refuses a single job title. The programme connects composition, Harikatha, teaching and research, showing how one person’s curiosity can move between private practice, public performance and durable institutions.
